# M0-09 Cross-platform hydrophobic-motif transfer

## Purpose

The transfer inventory expands synthesis support. It does not improve the
biological oracle and it does not transfer biological labels.

FORGE operates over three distinct spaces:

- \(G\): structures the whole-lipid generator can propose;
- \(O\): structures the biological oracle can score within a declared
  applicability policy;
- \(R\): structures for which FORGE can construct a supported route.

The actionable search space is approximately \(G \cap O \cap R\). Sparse route
documentation can make \(R\) artificially narrow even when a tail is chemically
accessible. Cross-platform tail review is therefore intended to distinguish
genuinely difficult chemistry from missing encoded route knowledge.

## Scope boundary

The current paper does not attempt to reconstruct every LNPDB lipid through
Ugi chemistry or mine every reaction reported in LNPDB.

Tail motifs are the primary transfer object because hydrophobic backbones,
branching, unsaturation, and degradable architectures can often be separated
from a platform-specific handle. Each admitted record must preserve the exact
source attachment and then establish a separate route to a Ugi-compatible
aldehyde or isocyanide.

Heads follow a stricter policy. An external head may transfer intact only when
it is an exact, site-defined Ugi-compatible amine. Head-motif conversion is not
part of this pilot. Novel heads may still be generated by FORGE, but each must
pass reactive-site qualification and close through procurement or an upstream
route.

Source-lipid activity, formulation behavior, yield, and route evidence are
never inherited by a converted component or a new Ugi product.

The registry also separates two reactions that must not be conflated:

1. the source-platform reaction that actually made the reported component;
2. the proposed reaction that installs a Ugi-compatible aldehyde or
   isocyanide handle.

Every motif links to an actual source reaction with conditions, isolated
yield, analytical-evidence status, and a source locator. A deterministic
handle conversion is not counted as a reported reaction or a complete route.

## First platform: XH_2025

The first source is the XH_2025 asymmetric maleate series. The hash-pinned
supplement reports six named aliphatic alcohols and their use in a
DCC/DMAP-mediated esterification with
(Z)-4-((2-ethylhexyl)oxy)-4-oxobut-2-enoic acid. Reported isolated yields for
the six maleates range from 85% to 92%.

The pilot preserves the alcohol carbon and oxygen as mapped attachment atoms.
It does not treat the maleate final assembly as Ugi evidence.

| Source component | Common precursor | Class | Ugi disposition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D18 | (9Z,12Z)-octadeca-9,12-dien-1-ol | primary, doubly unsaturated | propose aldehyde |
| D9.2 | 3-undecanol | secondary | defer |
| D9.8 | 9-heptadecanol | secondary | defer |
| D11.1 | 2-dodecanol | secondary | defer |
| D11.10 | 11-heneicosanol | secondary | defer |
| D12.8 | 2-octyldodecan-1-ol | primary, branched | propose aldehyde |

A deterministic primary-alcohol-to-aldehyde realization produces two candidate
Ugi inputs:

- `CCCCC/C=C\C/C=C\CCCCCCCC=O`
- `CCCCCCCCCCC(C=O)CCCCCCCC`

Both contain one qualified aldehyde handle, produce one unique product with
the frozen Ugi transform and fixed anchor reactants, and are absent by exact
identity from the current 62-aldehyde virtual component pool. Their nearest
ECFP4 Tanimoto similarities to that pool are 0.864 and 0.458, respectively.

The four secondary alcohols are not oxidized into aldehyde proposals because
that operation would yield ketones. They remain structure-only until an exact
or well-supported alternative route to a Ugi-compatible handle is admitted.
This is classified as missing route knowledge, not an observed chemical
failure.

## Second platform: JL_2024

The second source is the JL_2024 A3-coupling library. The hash-pinned article
and supplement report a DCC/DMAP esterification of hept-6-ynoic acid with five
named primary alcohols. The reaction used equimolar acid and alcohol, one
equivalent of DCC, 0.3 equivalents of DMAP, dry dichloromethane, room
temperature, and 12 hours, followed by aqueous workup and silica
chromatography.

| Source component | Common precursor | Source yield | Motif class |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| B12 | dodecan-1-ol | 86% | linear saturated |
| B13 | dec-9-en-1-ol | 98% | terminally unsaturated |
| B14 | undec-10-en-1-ol | 62% | terminally unsaturated |
| B15 | (Z)-non-2-en-1-ol | 77% | allylic, internal cis unsaturation |
| B16 | 2-decyltetradecan-1-ol | 99% | very-long-chain, alpha-branched |

The supplement gives exact product structures, isolated yields, proton and
carbon NMR, and high-resolution mass spectrometry for B12 through B16. The
alcohol attachment is therefore source-resolved rather than inferred from a
generic tail subgraph.

Oxidation of the five alcohols produces five structurally valid aldehyde
proposals. Each has one aldehyde handle, gives one product under the frozen
Ugi transform with fixed anchors, and is absent by exact identity from the
current 62-aldehyde virtual pool.

One proposal now closes computationally under the declared evidence policy.
An exact literature procedure converts 2-decyl-1-tetradecanol, the B16 alcohol
precursor, to 2-decyltetradecanal using 3.03 equivalents of pyridinium
chlorochromate in anhydrous dichloromethane at room temperature for 3 hours.
The aldehyde was isolated in 86% yield and identified by proton and carbon NMR.
The exact alcohol, TCI America D5283, was listed at greater than 97% purity with
current US stock and same-day shipment from Portland when checked on
29 July 2026. The thesis PDF and all computational inputs are hash-pinned.

This establishes one exact alcohol-to-aldehyde route to an accepted terminal
and one frozen-transform-compatible Ugi input. It does not establish that the
aldehyde will undergo the prospective Ugi reaction, that the final lipid will
be isolated, or that it will formulate or function biologically. The other six
aldehyde proposals remain incomplete. B15 retains an
alpha,beta-unsaturated-aldehyde compatibility flag, and B16 retains an
alpha-branched-aldehyde Ugi-scope flag and a pyridinium-chlorochromate process
burden.

## Route-outcome taxonomy

Every route attempt must terminate in one of four top-level categories:

1. complete route found;
2. chemically implausible or incompatible;
3. outside declared support;
4. missing route knowledge.

More specific gap labels are nested under category 4. This prevents absence of
an encoded transformation from being interpreted as experimental
unsynthesizability.

## Operational stopping rule

LNPDB defines and prioritizes the tail-motif census. General chemistry
literature, patents, supplier documents, and close analogues may provide the
best route evidence for the corresponding Ugi components.

Curation proceeds through four tiers:

1. close every permitted native Ugi-3 component and locked control;
2. transfer high-value motifs from other lipid platforms;
3. fill repeated route-knowledge gaps exposed by biologically competitive,
   oracle-supported generated candidates;
4. leave chemistry irrelevant to the declared paper scope uncurated.

The stopping objective is operational closure:

> Stop when missing route knowledge no longer materially constrains the
> biologically relevant Ugi-3 design space under the declared support and
> oracle-applicability policies.

The final saturation protocol measures weighted motif coverage,
candidate-route closure, marginal gain across consecutive curation rounds, and
prospective-panel dossier closure. Exact numerical thresholds are frozen in
`configs/route/m0_09_l2_supervision_decision.json` and summarized in
`docs/M0_09_L2_SUPERVISION_DECISION.md`.

## Reuse beyond the Ugi instantiation

The transferable platform asset is the common synthesis-program
infrastructure: route trees, reaction records, reusable precursor subroutes,
evidence and availability states, forward checks, failure taxonomy, and
synthesis-value coupling. A future A3, maleate, aza-Michael, or
epoxide-opening instantiation can reuse those objects. It must still add its
own final-assembly adapter, reactant roles, substrate-scope evidence,
chemistry-specific supervision, and prospective validation.
